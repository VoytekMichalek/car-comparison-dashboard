# Comparative repair exposure — 18 October 2026

This follow-up audits the eleven non-Tesla model families against the repair fund already in the dashboard. It addresses unexpected mechanical/electrical repairs and uncovered component wear. Scheduled maintenance, tyres and insured collision damage belong in their existing separate budgets.

## Finding

No public Australian dataset found in this audit establishes a comparable, age-adjusted annual **owner-paid unscheduled repair cost** for these exact configurations and the Model Y. A component price establishes the size of a possible bill, not how often it will occur. Warranty repairs and recall rectification may inconvenience a family but should not automatically be charged to the owner’s repair fund.

The evidence does not establish that Tesla must have the highest expected repair bill. Toyota’s smaller conventional-hybrid packs have an established Australian aftermarket, while the other BEVs and PHEVs can also present substantial electrical/thermal costs once coverage ends. New EV5, EVO, Sealion 6 Extended and Zeekr 7X cars lack a long local history; that is uncertainty, rather than proof of low or high reliability.

The existing non-Tesla point allowances should remain provisional **savings contributions**, not newly relabelled Australian averages. This audit does not justify a precise numerical increase in any of those points. Do not raise them merely to make a brand ranking look plausible. The root Tesla audit can separately correct inconsistencies among the Tesla configurations.

## Material new coverage finding: early Ioniq 5 / EV6 ICCU

Australia’s official [Hyundai recall REC-006224](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006224) covers selected 2021–24 Ioniq 5/6 VINs and promises a free software update. It supersedes REC-005942. The [Kia recall REC-006238](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006238) covers selected 2021–24 EV6 VINs and explicitly includes ICCU inspection/replacement where required plus software, free of charge; it supersedes REC-005946. The number of recalled vehicles is not the number of units that failed.

A further Australian extension was reported in May 2026. [Drive’s manufacturer-attributed report](https://www.drive.com.au/news/hyundai-kia-and-genesis-extend-charging-unit-warranty-in-australia-for-some-evs/) states **15 years / 300,000 km from first registration** for Ioniq 5 built **2 April 2021–14 February 2024**, and EV6 **MY22–MY24**. The Australian Electric Vehicle Association also [shared that Australian announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aevanational_vital-component-of-some-hyundai-kia-and-activity-7457003066857517056-6_Mj). The direct Drive page was unavailable to the research fetcher; a syndicated copy exposed its explicit Hyundai Australia spokesperson and Kia Australia attribution. General Australian manufacturer warranty pages do not display a downloadable ICCU-specific extension contract found during this check. Treat it as a **reported Australian extension, obtain VIN-specific written confirmation**. Do not apply it to the 84-kWh facelift or to EV5.

This materially weakens an assumption that a covered early Ioniq 5 or EV6 owner should routinely pay for an ICCU replacement. No verified public Australian fitted ICCU tariff was found. Overseas parts prices, US warranty invoices, anonymous forum claims and AI-generated repair guides do not become Australian installed quotes. Keeping a conditional paid stress case is reasonable, but its coverage caveat must be prominent.

## What should change in presentation / methodology

- Call the column **Repair savings** or **Repair fund**. Explain that this is money set aside, not forecast expenditure. Keep it yellow and editable.
- An unused fund remains the owner’s money. Show how much of a ten-year budget is contributions, rather than implying it has all been spent on repairs.
- A new car’s lower present-day exposure cannot be held constant for ten years and described as an age-adjusted repair forecast. A flat amount can still be useful if explicitly a level contribution that builds a balance before warranty expiry. Do not fabricate a year-by-year failure curve to fix the limitation.
- A large stress case may exceed the accumulated fund. Preserve separate, scope-labelled repair examples rather than automatically adding an entire replacement pack to every car’s ten-year running total.
- Warranty eligibility, condition, prior commercial use, service records and the exact car’s purchase odometer matter more than small unsupported differences between these rounded amounts. Where those inputs are unknown, acknowledge them.

## Excluded / unresolved evidence

- Anonymous Australian posts mentioning$11,000 ICCU warranty repairs or phone-quoted hybrid packs do not establish retail owner-paid bills or failure rates.
- US/Canadian prices, RepairPal and UK reliability/repair surveys do not directly establish Queensland-dollar exposure; no currency conversion was added as new Australian evidence.
- Generic AutoGuru pages describe engine overheating or compressor belts for pure EVs. Such templates are unsuitable for a specific Ioniq5/EV6 thermal-system quote.
- Australian Electric Vehicle Specialists’ cached Toyota shop page lists a family-wide$1,790–5,690 range with37 options, but the fresh page says its store is not open. No individual Kluger price is established by that family-wide range.
- [EV Battery Lab](https://evbatterylab.com.au/hybrid-battery-replacement-cost) relays Mitsubishi’s historical$15,000–16,000 RRP for2022-onward Outlander packs, attributed to a September2024 [Drive report](https://www.drive.com.au/caradvice/how-long-does-a-hybrid-battery-last-and-how-much-does-it-cost-to-replace/). The original report could not be opened; scope/tax/labour are insufficiently verified for a new installed-price assertion. Keep it as a lead rather than silently replacing a stress-case quote.
- No credible Australian frequency-weighted annual repair series was found for EV5, EVO,7X, Sealion6, current e-Power, new RAV4 or Sorento HEV/PHEV. Narrow-looking annual ranges would be false precision.

## Child-seat evidence

All three entries use `tested-related`: published three-seat evidence exists for the same generation, but a different grade, year or powertrain was tested. This is enough to justify a trial with the family’s seats. It does not certify three arbitrary forward-facing seats or boosters.

- **S5 Forester:** BabyDrive’s [2019 Premium test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2019-subaru-forester/) fitted a rear-facing Britax Unity capsule and rear-facing Graphene outside, with a forward-facing Maxi Guard Pro in the centre. All three were installed using the vehicle belts to gain space. The reviewed 2.5i-L years/grade are related; no exact three-booster buckle test was verified.
- **S6 Forester:** BabyDrive’s [2025 Hybrid Sport test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2025-subaru-forester-hybrid-review/) fitted three forward-facing seats, or two forward-facing plus a rear-facing seat behind the driver. The article does not identify every restraint model. The dashboard explicitly retains the hybrid/grade difference from base petrol.
- **BT Outback:** BabyDrive’s [2021 Sport test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/all-new-2021-subaru-outback-sport/) fitted two forward-facing and one rear-facing restraint. The article does not supply model names. The roof-mounted centre belt was awkward; three-booster buckle access remains untested.

All have two outer ISOFIX positions and three rear top-tether positions. The user’s own fit stays pending. No third row is present.

Safety generations remain separate: [S5 Forester 2019 assessment](https://www.ancap.com.au/safety-ratings/subaru/forester/1f3f86), [S6 Forester 2024 assessment](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn.ancap.com.au/app/public/assets/1505a0e2c855214de7c9e2a3f0aef902297797ac/original.pdf?1767074059=), and [BT Outback 2021 assessment](https://www.ancap.com.au/safety-ratings/subaru/outback/c5ee38). Equal star counts do not imply equal test protocols.

## Ownership inputs and their limits

Manufacturer private-use warranty is five years/unlimited kilometres from original supply, with different commercial-use limits. A used purchase does not restart it. The [official warranty handbook](https://docs.subaru.com.au/3194-warranty_service_handbook_5_year_cps_sa1180_v6.pdf) and model-specific documentation take precedence over dealer extended-warranty advertisements.

**Servicing:** S5 and BT non-XT use 12 months/12,500 km, whichever first. [MY24 Forester](https://docs.subaru.com.au/MY24_Forester_Capped_Price_Servicing.pdf) first five visits total $2,674.64 and [MY25 non-XT Outback](https://docs.subaru.com.au/MY25-Subaru-Outback-%28excluding-XT%29-Capped-Price-Servicing.pdf) total $2,715.67 over 62,500 km. At the dashboard’s 15,000 km/year, a sixth visit can arise inside five years. The annual $750 is an explicit used/post-cap planning allowance, not a capped quote for every old car.

The [model-specific MY26 Forester schedule](https://docs.subaru.com.au/MY26-Subaru-Forester-Capped-Price-Servicing.pdf) states 12 months/15,000 km and five visits of $347.95, $520.45, $393.10, $679.30 and $358.20: $2,299 total, or $459.80/year at 15,000 km/year. The generic service website still lists Forester at 12,500 km; the exact MY26 document is used. Later service years remain unquoted.

**Insurance:** [Youi’s Subaru page](https://www.youi.com.au/car-insurance/insurance-by-make/subaru) reports national averages of $1,069 Forester and $1,138 Outback for policies sold 22 November 2025–22 May 2026. They are family-wide observations, rounded for planning, not Queensland quotes for the user’s postcode, driver or year/grade. Broad editable bounds are editorial, not statistical confidence intervals. The same family average on new and old Foresters is a visible limitation.

**Tyres:** JAX regular fitted Michelin Primacy 5 anchors were $299 each for [225/60R17](https://www.jaxtyres.com.au/tyres/sizes/17-inch/225-60r17), $299 for [225/55R18](https://www.jaxtyres.com.au/tyres/sizes/18-inch/225-55r18) and $309 for [225/60R18](https://www.jaxtyres.com.au/tyres/sizes/18-inch/225-60r18). Fitting, balancing, valves, disposal and GST are included; alignment is separate. The replacement load indices meet/exceed the standard placard examples; confirm the actual placard and local stock. The 45,000 km life assumption, bounded 30,000–60,000 km, is editorial and editable rather than measured wear evidence.

**Repairs:** No representative Australian configuration-specific failure probabilities or installed CVT/engine repair quotes were found. The annual amounts are low-confidence savings budgets, **not an observed average** or insurance premium. The lower new-car reserve reflects initial manufacturer cover and age; it is not evidence that S6 has proved more reliable. Major failures can exceed the fund. Scheduled service, tyres and insured collision repairs are separate.

The official [US thermo-control-valve bulletin 09-119-24](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-10253930-0001.pdf) identifies selected 2019–21 Forester and 2020–22 Outback vehicles. It supports checking an early used car’s diagnostic and replacement history. Its US 15-year/150,000-mile extension is **not** represented as an Australian entitlement, nor its incidence transferred to all Australian cars or the new S6. Australian installed pricing stays explicitly unpriced.

For BT Outback, [REC-001227/SRT306](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-001227) and [REC-005005/SRT315](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-005005) cover selected MY21 EyeSight software VINs; applicable official rectification is free. The new [REC-006737/SRT390](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006737), published 7 October 2026 for 42 Forester Hybrid VINs, is explicitly excluded from this petrol S6 entry. All recall matching remains VIN-specific.

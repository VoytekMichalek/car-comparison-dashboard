# Research and data maintenance

## Per-model deep audits

The current family target is **one rear-facing restraint plus two forward-facing seats or boosters**, across rows where supported. Exact seats remain unspecified. Do not reject that mixed arrangement merely because three forward-facing seats failed a test.

`src/data/research/<familyId>.json` holds each dedicated model audit: sourced corrections, ten item-level findings and one detailed fit assessment per configuration. `src/research.ts` validates these packs and applies updates by stable ID before the main schemas validate the final dataset. Arrays replace; objects merge recursively. An unknown update target or duplicate update is an error. Do not edit both the base and an audit override without checking which value wins. The companion reports in `docs/research/` record methods and unresolved evidence.

Notebook v1 now includes an optional `fitTarget` marker for the one-rear-facing plus two-other-restraint target. Loading or importing an older backup clears only legacy fit confirmations and marks `fitReviewNeeded` when any were true; notes, listings, costs and seat-model text survive. The UI explains the reset and permits dismissal. Current-target confirmations round-trip normally. Unsupported target markers are rejected without replacing existing storage. Never infer a new confirmation from a previous target or a published test.

Family-fit scores use arrangement 45%, booster buckles 25%, cabin/restraint clearance 20% and loading/access 10%. A positive practical finding scores 9, a demonstrated fit with constraints 6, and a serious restriction 2. Unknown criteria are excluded from the raw average and reduce the category’s evidence factor. Arrangement must be established before any score is given; a restricted arrangement caps the category at 2. Exact scope multiplies known-criterion coverage by 80%, related scope by 50%. Personal fit confirmation remains 10 with full influence. These are disclosed editorial judgments, not measured safety scores or proof that the user's seats fit.

Optional `observedConsumption` and `observedRange` facts preserve matched road tests separately from official claims. BEV ownership energy budgets prefer a matched observed consumption value with a known measurement boundary; personal annual energy overrides still take priority. A mains/wall figure includes charging losses. Battery-side figures are adjusted once by the editable loss assumption. Unknown boundaries do not enter this observed-value path. Observed range does not replace WLTP/NEDC or determine the range score. Tests performed in another trim, battery or generation must retain their scope limitation.

## Add or refresh a configuration

1. Read the Australian manufacturer brochure/manual for that generation, drivetrain and model year. Preserve the actual source URL; current model pages can change under old URLs. Record dealer-hosted copies of manufacturer documents as such.
2. Read the applicable ANCAP report. Store the assessment year and build/variant applicability, not just stars. Newer variants do not automatically inherit ratings. Restraint-installation assessments are not simultaneous three-across tests.
3. Find independent Australian family tests. Record the exact seats, facing directions, trim, generation and any buckle/access restrictions. `tested-three` and `tested-multirow` require evidence for the populated configuration; reference tests of another trim/powertrain use `tested-related`, with lower scoring evidence. Combination restrictions use `limitations`. Neither status confirms the user’s seats.
4. Check manufacturer warranty/service terms and official Australian recall records. Distinguish original warranty from current new-car terms and VIN-specific coverage. Warranty eligibility may depend on servicing, ownership and use.
5. Capture asking-price evidence, condition, fee basis, location and odometer. Record the actual observed sample count and date. Indexed or ambiguous offers must retain their limitations; use `basis: "unverified"` when charges or applicability cannot be established. Do not call a planning band an observed market range. Explicit best estimates use `kind: "estimate"`, `sampleCount: 0`, confidence and a calculation basis; the original observed anchors remain cited.
6. Add concise paraphrased strengths, compromises, year changes, inspection questions and explicit remaining gaps. Attach source IDs to each fact and relevant commentary group.
7. Add a correctly licensed photograph for the pictured generation, or retain the labelled photo fallback. Save it locally and preserve credit/license/source metadata. Overseas reference cars must say so. For a new generation, extend `imageFor()` instead of reusing an inaccurate image.
8. Run `npm run validate:data`, `npm test` and `npm run build`; check the affected detail views, comparison and costs.

## Facts, units and uncertainty

Each numerical `Fact` carries `value`, `unit`, `basis` and `sourceIds`. Missing values are `null`, never zero or a guessed average. Explain the gap in `basis` and the configuration's `gaps` list. Required units are km, kWh, kW, L, mm, kg, kWh/100km and L/100km.

- **Range:** distinguish WLTP, NEDC and observed results. Record wheel/trim conditions. PHEV range is electric-only; a non-plug-in hybrid has no equivalent electric-range claim here. The range-label helper uses the affirmative standard, not a later phrase such as “not WLTP”.
- **Consumption:** HEV consumption is combined petrol use. PHEV `consumption` is electrical use; `depletedConsumption` is petrol use after depletion. Never substitute a low, weighted PHEV fuel figure for depleted consumption.
- **Boot:** record the occupied/folded-row arrangement and measurement method. Tesla's non-VDA cargo claim is not directly equivalent to VDA litre figures, and frunk/underfloor inclusion must be clear.
- **Charging:** separate the vehicle's peak from the charger's rating. A quoted 10–80% time is conditional and is not guaranteed by peak power alone. Record connector type, AC phase conditions and any missing applicability evidence.
- **Fit:** dimensions, seat count and anchors alone do not demonstrate a safe three-seat combination. Booster buckle access deserves separate attention. Personal confirmations are private user records, not edits to published evidence.

## Ownership costs and review scoring

Additional research lives in the `*-ownership.json`, `*-ownership-sources.json`, `*-reviews.json`, `*-review-sources.json`, `qld-costs.json`, `qld-cost-sources.json`, `brands.json` and `brand-sources.json` files. Dates, service eligibility, original versus current warranty terms, repair quote inclusions and owner-review scope must remain explicit. Never infer a model’s failure rate from forum posts, recall counts or national origin.

QLD registration uses actual cylinder class: BEVs and the three-cylinder X-Trail e-Power share the lower group. Other populated hybrids use four-cylinder fees. CTP is added once; an owner’s full registration override replaces registration plus CTP. Duty above $100,000 uses the higher rate for the entire value, not just the excess. Dutiable value is transaction-specific; an asking-price illustration is not an official assessment. Drive-away duty/transfer costs are not added again.

Insurance and service start with explicit model/class estimates and low/high sensitivity ranges. `insurance-estimates.json`, `budget-benchmarks.json` and `budget-estimate-sources.json` record the primary anchors and all planning adjustments. The insurance anchors include a published Brisbane reference profile; they are not household quotes. Anonymous public planning defaults are stored in `insurance-defaults.json`; the original Tesla Model Y RWD starts at A$2,000/year, with yellow uncertainty and an illustrative sensitivity range. These are not personal quotes. New per-car or shared browser-local inputs take priority. Service budgets explain age/plan eligibility and unknowns. Tyres use the configuration-specific set-price and assumed-life ranges in `tyre-estimates.json`, scaled by annual kilometres; repair reserves use the per-configuration defaults described below. Both are editable allowances, not expected annual bills. A home charger starts at A$2,050 for plug-ins and an inspection at A$385; users can set them to zero. Finance inputs are annual interest/fees, excluding principal. Purchase costs and depreciation remain separate from annual operating subtotals.

Electrical `Fact.energyBasis` is `wall`, `battery` or `unspecified`. WLTP mains consumption already includes charging losses. Extra assumed losses apply once to battery-side facts or explicitly labelled battery-side planning proxies. The original Tesla observed figure has an unverified metering boundary. BYD’s PHEV brochure values do not establish electric-distance versus weighted consumption. The original facts remain unverified for calculation purposes. Separate `OwnershipInfo.energyEstimate` / `depletedEstimate` ranges provide low-confidence planning proxies; owner-entered budgets take priority.

`src/scoring.ts` holds the eight public scoring rubrics and evidence factors. Category weights are integers 1–10; defaults are seating10, purchase9, running8, boot7, electric-range6, safety8, community-reviews6 and brand-support6. Effective weight = `importance * evidenceFactor`; overall = `sum(rawScore * effectiveWeight) / sum(effectiveWeight)`. Missing evidence contributes zero weight, with no neutral-5 substitute. Non-applicable categories are excluded from both scoring and coverage. Applicable missing categories reduce displayed evidence coverage; no usable evidence produces a null total. This is an editorial decision tool, not an objective safety/reliability model.

Electric range is scored **only for battery-only EVs (BEVs)** using the existing 50–600 km scale and WLTP/NEDC evidence factors. PHEVs have petrol backup; their intentionally shorter electric-only range is informational, not a low BEV score. Their range category is non-applicable with `score: null`, zero effective weight and a **Not scored** label; the published km and range standard remain visible. It contributes neither to the overall average nor to the applicable-importance denominator for evidence coverage. Changing range importance from 1 to 10 therefore cannot change a PHEV total or coverage, including in the price-versus-score chart. PHEV running budgets still use the separate editable electric-driving share. Conventional hybrids, petrol and diesel remain excluded from range scoring; BEV scoring is unchanged.

The running-budget rubric uses a fixed linear scale: `10 - (annualBudget - 4000) * 9 / 4000`, clamped to 1–10. A$4,000/year or less scores 10; A$8,000/year or more scores 1. These editorial reference points cover the catalogue’s current roughly A$4,500–8,100 default budgets. They are not percentiles, and filters or selected comparison cars never change them. Invalid or incomplete budgets receive no score. The earlier A$2,000–6,500 scale was stale after full ownership components were added. After the budget-tyre update, original Model Y RWD defaults score 6.6/10 from A$5,525.15/year including A$1,000 repair savings; A$4,525.15 is the separate subtotal before repair savings. Personal cost inputs affect the budget used by this rubric; evidence factors affect its weight, not its displayed raw score.

Community scores use the published /5 aggregate, count and scope. Their evidence factor is capped `n/(n+20)` times the explicit scope factor in `src/reviewScope.ts`. A single GT-Line delivery review barely influences an EV5 Air assessment; original Atto3, early Ioniq5 and XA50 RAV4 ratings do not score incompatible newer generations. Scope factors are editorial relevance judgments, not measured reliability probabilities. The deep audit reduces mixed-generation or borrowed-car aggregates, including Outlander, Model Y, EV6 and Ioniq 5. Brand support uses documented Australian service access, separately from owner sentiment and mechanical failure claims. ANCAP protocol years remain visible.

Yellow styling identifies automatic estimates, uncertain purchase amounts and repair proxies; each is backed by a range or explicit basis. Actual quote inputs take priority, including zero. Future resale is a scenario, using purchase × (1 − annual depreciation rate)^years, with 8–25% BEV, 7–20% PHEV and 5–16% HEV sensitivity rates. These are assumptions informed by resale evidence, not published forecasts. Running-cost score confidence is limited by the weakest of the energy, insurance, servicing, registration, tyres and repair-reserve evidence factors, so filled-in estimates do not become certain evidence.

Shared URLs include weights, while cost quotes and personal fit checks remain private. Those local inputs can change a recipient’s score. The optional ownership/weight sections extend v1 notebook backups without invalidating original v1 exports.

## Multi-site reviews and charts — 18 October 2026

`src/data/review-expansion/*.json` separates owner cohorts, professional rating indices and reliability studies. `src/reviewData.ts` validates the strict contract. Every family needs coverage/gaps and every configuration needs an explicit applicability match, including factor zero for incompatible data. Each cohort records the original rating scale, numeric denominator and denominator type, country, model/engine/generation scope, overlap group, source and actual checked date. The first source must be the original rating publisher page; its domain identifies duplicate publisher cohorts. Do not use a count of text comments as the denominator of a separately calculated numeric rating. Preserve search-only figures as gaps rather than verified aggregates.

A freshly researched owner cohort replaces a legacy aggregate when its canonical source URL matches, including when the replacement is reference-only. This prevents a newly identified contaminated rating from leaking back into scores. A known mixed engine, obsolete generation or non-vehicle service aggregate is reference-only when its appropriate denominator cannot be isolated. International related cars receive reduced editorial scope factors. Australian-only counts require an exactly Australia-only region; a mixed AU/NZ pool is not all Australian.

`src/reviewEvidence.ts` converts eligible owner ratings to /10. Source evidence is `min(0.8, n/(n+20)) * scopeFactor`. Only the strongest applicable cohort per publisher and overlap group contributes. The owner sentiment mean uses those evidence weights, while the entire category's evidence is the strongest contributing source. Adding sites therefore does not automatically inflate confidence. Count totals are contributions across sites, **not unique owners**. Professional reviews, expert meta-ratings and quality surveys remain visible separately and never enter the owner sentiment average. More than 50 verified matching contributions is not available for every car; never manufacture a denominator to reach a target.

The Charts hash route (`#view=charts`) uses the same public filters, selected family configurations, weights and browser-local purchase/resale inputs as the rest of the app. No runtime data API is used. `src/charts.ts` supplies both graph models. Depreciation is a **powertrain planning scenario**, not a fitted model forecast: value in year t is purchase * (1 - annualRate)^t, rounded to A$100 after purchase. Low/high rates give sensitivity bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. An explicit resale input anchors its configured ownership year exactly; other points follow the implied compound rate. A zero-resale anchor uses a labelled quadratic decline to zero, because finite compound rates cannot reach zero. An anchor implying appreciation is shown as an assumption, not silently clamped to depreciation. Past the saved anchor, the same implied curve is extrapolated. The graph horizon does not change the user's ownership period.

The scatter uses `ownershipEstimate().purchasePrice` for x and re-summarises `scoreVehicle()` categories with purchase price completely removed for y. The purchase-price category contributes neither score weight nor coverage denominator on this graph. Other category weights/evidence are unchanged; Scores & reviews retains its ordinary purchase-inclusive total. There is no positional jitter. Colour is powertrain; hollow dots are provisional. Unknown price/score pairs are withheld. Fee bases remain attached, and a car selector resolves overlapping dots. Only one selected configuration per family is plotted; choose variants on Browse. ### Chart readability and purchase percentages

Each annual depreciation point records signed percentage change from the actual selected purchase price: `(yearValue - purchasePrice) / purchasePrice * 100`. Zero purchase has no meaningful percentage and returns null. The card, selected year, per-car legend and all-car ledger show these changes; they are not depreciation from original new MSRP.

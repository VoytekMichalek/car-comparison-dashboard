## Specifications and year applicability

The [manufacturer’s Australian 22.5MY Outlander brochure, hosted by a dealer](https://resource.digitaldealer.com.au/pdf/53717918630e8a6b59b77948323546.pdf) identifies the LS FWD seven-seat configuration: 7.7 L/100 km combined ADR 81/02, 91-RON fuel, 478 L VDA with row three folded and 163 L VDA with it up, 4,710 × 1,862 mm body dimensions, 1,600 kg braked towing and 235/60R18 tyres. The 7.5 L/100 km ES five-seat figure is not transferred. This is a period document, not the current facelift brochure. Current manufacturer historical brochure URLs can return errors, so the working period mirror is recorded.

[Nissan’s Australian MY23 specification sheet](https://www.nissan.com.au/content/dam/Nissan/AU/Files/Brochures/Models/Specsheet/NIS4635_Nissan_MY23_X-TRAIL_POS_Update_eSpec_v3_web.pdf) gives the petrol AWD seven-seater 7.8 L/100 km ADR combined, 91 RON, 2,000 kg braked towing, 235/65R17 tyres and a 465-L cargo figure with row three folded **measured to the roof**. It is not directly comparable with the Outlander’s VDA-to-seatback figure. Nissan’s 2,065-mm width includes mirrors; its basis is explicit. All-seven-up litres remain unknown rather than importing an unsupported number.

[Nissan’s MY26 announcement](https://australia.nissannews.com/en-AU/releases/my26-nissan-x-trail-now-on-sale-in-australia) confirms the service interval changes from 10,000 km/12 months to 15,000 km/12 months, and ST gains a 12.3-inch screen with wireless phone mirroring. These changes justify excluding MY26 from the original cohort. Both entries use null electric range, traction battery and charging fields; petrol consumption is L/100 km, with no PHEV weighted figure.

## Family fit: evidence and limits

[BabyDrive’s 2022 Exceed seven-seat test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2022-mitsubishi-outlander-exceed-seven-seater/) reports three forward-facing Infasecure Achieve More, Aspire More and Attain More restraints. This supports a related-trim LS candidate, not a guaranteed LS or user-seat fit.

[BabyDrive’s 2023 seven-seat T33 test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2023-nissan-x-trail-seven-seater-review/) used Achieve More and Momentum More forward-facing plus an Adapt More rear-facing capsule. The exact trim is not confirmed. It is not recast as a three-forward-facing or three-booster test.

The [Outlander technical report](https://cdn.ancap.com.au/app/public/assets/e9e2fecc6529d6359a9ffaeb24ea46dd625b1414/original.pdf?1758674620=) and [X-Trail technical report](https://cdn.ancap.com.au/app/public/assets/341bb460aff177d7bb1b54c12cc468e72797fc23/original.pdf?1720509150=) show two outboard ISOFIX positions, three row-two top tethers and **no third-row top tethers**. ANCAP does not recommend child restraints in either third row. Its individual-position tests also found some centre-seat restraint/booster combinations incompatible; a successful BabyDrive combination does not override those restrictions. Both records use `tested-related`, with the user’s fit check pending. Both tests describe removing a row-two restraint to access row three. Dedicated third-row ventilation is absent.

The public X-Trail technical table lists third-row side-head airbags as unavailable, while manufacturer/advert descriptions can imply curtain coverage there. The dataset records the conflict and asks for the specific Australian manual/VIN confirmation; it makes no affirmative third-row protection claim. Outlander’s original test also identifies absent third-row curtain coverage. Seven seating positions alone do not establish three suitable restraint positions using row three.

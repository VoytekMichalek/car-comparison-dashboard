# Budget replacement tyres: ICE sizes and service life

Implementation decisions and final figures: [consolidated update](BUDGET-TYRES-UPDATE-2026-10-18.md). The integrated dashboard uses a common 40,000 km life assumption with 25,000–60,000 km sensitivity; earlier recommendations below are research notes, not the final defaults.

Checked **18 October 2026**. Australian consumer retail pages; no quote request or purchase submitted. Prices are snapshots, not guaranteed Brisbane stock or future prices. A retailer listing is evidence of price and specification, not an independent endorsement of wet braking or durability.

## Finding

The existing Michelin/Continental premium anchors substantially overstate a budget replacement strategy. Lower-priced fitted tyres exist even at the conservative load/speed ratings currently used in the dashboard. Retain the vehicle's exact size and check its actual placard; a premium replacement's higher load index is not proof of the car's minimum requirement.

## Tyre-life evidence and what it cannot establish

[Bridgestone Australia](https://www.bridgestone.com.au/Learn/Maintenance/Age-of-a-tyre) gives a broad **40,000–80,000 km** range for most passenger tyres with normal driving and good care; performance/heavy-use tyres can wear faster. It identifies driving, vehicle, road conditions and maintenance as variables, not model-specific average lifetimes. This does not prove budget Landspiders will last 80,000 km.

[Michelin Australia EV FAQ](https://www.michelin.com.au/auto/advice/ev-guide/electric-vehicles-faq) says regular tyres can function on EVs when load and speed requirements are met. EV-specific options can offer noise/range/wear benefits. Its [2024 EV advice](https://www.michelin.com.au/auto/advice/ev-guide/electric-car-tyres) notes purpose-designed EV tyre sizing can make wear similar to equivalent ICE vehicles; torque and weight alone do not establish a fixed lifespan penalty for each EV.

[Tyre Stewardship Australia's commissioned research](https://storage.googleapis.com/tsa_craftcms_media/assets/pdf-resources/TSA-EVs-and-End-of-Life-Tyres-Report.pdf), printed pages 17–21 (PDF 22–26), reviews higher weight, acceleration and wear. It stresses evidence limitations and, in section 4.3.2, says specific evidence supporting the prediction of more frequent EV tyre replacement could not be found. It supplies **no Tesla 35,000 km, other-EV 40,000 km or ICE 45,000 km model averages**. Avoid converting particulate emission estimates into replacement kilometres.

[Michelin tyre-life advice](https://www.michelin.com.au/auto/advice/change-tyres/how-long-do-tyres-last) recommends professional annual inspections after five years in use and precautionary replacement at ten years from manufacture. Age, damage and wear can require replacement independent of kilometres. A distance-only allowance does not cover every low-mileage scenario.

**Implementation recommendation:** use a common, editable **40,000 km** planning life, with **25,000–60,000 km** sensitivity bounds, until direct model/tyre-combination evidence is available. This is deliberately an assumption. Alternatively retain 40,000/45,000 EV/ICE assumptions with explicit low confidence, but there is insufficient primary evidence for Tesla alone to default to 35,000 km. At 15,000 km/year the common-life annual allowance is set price × 0.375: suggested values above imply approximately **A$263, A$263, A$300 and A$338/year**. These are savings allowances, not a replacement invoice every year.

Do not increase predicted life just because a product is cheaper; do not claim equal tyre performance from equal size/load/speed. Vehicle suspension condition, pressure, rotation, alignment, driving and actual tyre selection remain unresolved inputs. Scheduled servicing, tyres and unexpected repairs should remain separate to prevent double-counting.

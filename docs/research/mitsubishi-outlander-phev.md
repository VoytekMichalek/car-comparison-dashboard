# Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV: individual audit

Checked 18 October 2026. Two configurations: original **20 kWh, five-seat Aspire (2022–25)** and updated **22.7 kWh, seven-seat Aspire (2026 listings / Australian MY25 brochure)**. The household target is **one rear-facing restraint plus two forward-facing seats or boosters**. Actual restraint models remain unknown.

## Findings that affect the decision

The original car has useful mixed-restraint evidence. [BabyDrive's April 2025 test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/electric-cars/2025-mitsubishi-outlander-phev-review/) used an ES five-seater, not Aspire: three seats fitted including a rear-facing infant capsule, with a reported 184–186 cm driver ahead. The full restraint models and locations are not stated. This supports **workable three-across**, with good capsule front clearance, rather than a universal fit guarantee. Booster-buckle convenience is unverified.

The [original ANCAP technical report, pages 3–4](https://cdn.ancap.com.au/app/public/assets/e9e2fecc6529d6359a9ffaeb24ea46dd625b1414/original.pdf?1758674620=) adds an important constraint: one selected convertible could not be correctly installed in either direction in the centre, and one selected booster also failed there. Its restraints were assessed individually, not as simultaneous three-across combinations. Do not infer a failure for every rear-facing combination from this finding.

The updated seven-seat Aspire has **no exact mixed three-seat test verified**. The [2025 ANCAP report](https://cdn.ancap.com.au/app/public/assets/a9a3ec37533de52f9c73446393cdc3001f9fcbf1/original.pdf?1758594945=) records no third-row top tethers and does not recommend installing restraints there. Its individual second-row checks do not establish three-across fit. The old ES observation remains context; unknown updated fit receives no positive or negative arrangement score.

The owner's published aggregate is not a clean sample. [ProductReview's ZM PHEV page](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/mitsubishi-outlander-4th-gen-zm-phev) displays **4.7/5 from four reviews**, but one explicitly describes a **2021 GSR purchased August 2021**, with 49 km electric range: the previous generation. The JSON now keeps that displayed aggregate in commentary while setting the scored owner rating to **unavailable**. No invented corrected three-owner average.

Both are 12-month/15,000-km schedules. A used purchase does not restart services or warranty. Earlier road-test launch prices are not substituted for the manufacturer's currently published VIN-cohort schedule.

The [updated exact schedule](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com.au/content/dam/mmal/pdfs/cps-updates/outlander/2026-04-01/25MY_Outlander_PHEV_4x4_24L_Purchased%20from%201%20April%202026_CPS_A4.pdf) lists A$377, 449, 399, 499, 399, 899, 399, 899, 499, 899. CarExpert's current article totals A$5,498 with several different intermediate services; the dated manufacturer's **A$5,718** is retained. Ask the dealer for the exact original-purchase/VIN schedule.

The [ZM PHEV maintenance schedule](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com.au/content/dam/mmal/pdfs/maintenance-schedules/22MY%20ZM%20Outlander%20Plug-In%20Hybrid%20service%20schedule%20%28WEB%29%20FINAL.pdf) makes severe use relevant: normal transaxle-oil replacement is 195,000 km/10 years; towing, camper/car-top-carrier or rough/muddy-road use changes it to 30,000 km/24 months. Engine coolant is first 165,000 km/8 years; rear-motor coolant has a much longer schedule. Do not invent an annual “battery coolant change” for this car.

[Full warranty terms](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com.au/content/dam/mmal/pdfs/diamond-advantage/Mitsubishi-Diamond-Advantage-Full-Terms-and-Conditions-October-2023.pdf) list shocks/struts, hoses, injectors, LED/HID globes and other wear items under **12 months/20,000 km**, and the original auxiliary battery under **12 months**. This is a useful distinction from the ten-year headline. LED-globe wording is not a claim that every lamp assembly failure is excluded. Australian Consumer Law rights are separate. [Battery-capacity terms](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com.au/owners/diamond-advantage/warranty.html) were freshly read.

Official recalls checked: [REC-005692](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-005692) has a free affected-VIN camera software remedy. [REC-005938](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-005938) is a selected MY22 IVI/camera VIN campaign; [REC-005634](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-005634) concerns missing button-battery warnings on selected keys/manuals. [REC-006322](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006322) names **Exceed/Exceed Tourer** steering-touch sensors; do not portray that as an Aspire-wide fault. No VIN supplied, so this is a research shortlist, not a recall-clearance certificate.

## Evidence boundaries

- The old [CarExpert Aspire test](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2023-mitsubishi-outlander-phev-aspire-review) supports **7.2 L/100 km depleted** over a roughly 50-km mixed loop, indicated rather than pump measured. Its 6.1 L/100 km over 200 km includes electric driving. The [updated Exceed test](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2026-mitsubishi-outlander-phev-review) reports 5.2 L/100 km plus 20.0 kWh/100 km in mixed use and used Charge mode. Neither becomes pure-electric purchased-energy or depleted fuel. The current **22 kWh/100 km electric-distance planning estimate** remains low confidence.
- No exact Australian fitted 20/22.7-kWh pack quote or defensible annual repair average was obtained. An independent site attributes A$15,000–16,000 RRP to Mitsubishi via a Drive report, but the original Drive page returned 403 and could not be verified. This secondary lead does not replace the app's explicitly uncertain pack scenario. US part prices, Canadian cold-weather anecdotes and old-generation battery problems were not applied as Australian failure rates.
- Australian owner-manual searches did not yield a verified exact current manual in this pass. A manufacturer portal search result was for Pajero Sport, not Outlander, and was rejected. An authorised dealer's “PHEV brochure” link actually returned a petrol brochure; it was not used. The original MY23 brochure URL now returns 404. Fresh MY24/MY25 maker PDFs and both ANCAP reports establish the facts above; the missing manual remains a fit-check gap.
- Research re-read every populated specification, price, family, warranty, service, insurance, tyre, repair and review field. It does not pretend to validate unknowns or invent a failure probability. Brand-wide trust figures were not altered because another agent owns Mitsubishi petrol research.

The older related physical fit remains recorded, but the arrangement rating is now unknown and the family-fit score withheld pending the exact Australian PHEV manual and written manufacturer clarification. The updated PHEV was already unverified. Do not infer Nissan restrictions from a shared platform.

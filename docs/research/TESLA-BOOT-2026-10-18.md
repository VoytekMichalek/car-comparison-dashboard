# Tesla Model Y boot capacity

Checked 18 October 2026. The Australian Model Y has published boot figures and strong local practical evidence. A missing VDA score must not be presented as missing boot-space data.

## Manufacturer figures

| Australian manual / five-seat configuration | Rear seats up, rear cargo only | Front boot, separate | Total with five occupants | Rear seats folded, rear only | Folded rear + front total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original 2020–2024 manual; applicable to the original-body 2022–2024 Australian cars, including 2023 RWD and Long Range | 854 L | 117 L | 971 L | 2,041 L | 2,158 L |
| Current 2025+ manual, **Premium five-seater and Performance**; refreshed body | 822 L | 116 L | 938 L | 2,022 L | 2,138 L |

These are Tesla's own approximate cargo figures. Neither cargo table identifies VDA or specifies the loading-height limit. Do not call 971 L / 938 L the rear boot: those totals include the front compartment. Do not count front or underfloor storage a second time. The current manual also contains a distinct six-seat Model Y L; its figures do not apply to the five-seater.

Sources read:

- [Original Australian owner manual: Dimensions](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/2020_2024_modely/en_au/GUID-E47C4A6D-528E-419C-8C57-FD3864644C34.html), cargo table.
- [Current Australian owner manual: Dimensions](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/modely/en_au/GUID-1E76B638-7B12-4D9A-8767-94B7F1E92A0E.html), Premium five-seater / Performance cargo table.

## Australian practical tests

[BabyDrive's 2023 test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/electric-cars/2023-tesla-model-y/) reports 15 shopping bags in the main rear boot, a twin side-by-side pram with five bags, or a single pram with seven. It separately fits three bags under the floor and four in the front boot. The tested prams and bags are practical observations, not a litre conversion.

[BabyDrive's 2025 refreshed RWD test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2025-tesla-model-y-review/) again reports 15 main-boot shopping bags. It fits a Thule Sleek single or sibling-seat configuration plus six bags, a single Urban Glide 3 plus four bags, or a double Urban Glide 3 without bags. A 90 L Thule duffel fits under the floor; four shopping bags fit in front. A fitting 90 L bag is not proof of a 90 L compartment.

[Wheels/WhichCar's original-body Australian review](https://www.whichcar.com.au/reviews/tesla-model-y-review) explicitly describes the 854 L as including the underfloor well and being measured to the roof, rather than the common rear-seatback-height comparison. Its folded 2,158 L statement includes the front compartment when reconciled to Tesla's table; use the primary manual's separated figures above.

## Independent measured context: not a replacement Australian VDA figure

[ADAC's refreshed Maximum Range RWD test, page 4](https://assets.adac.de/image/upload/v1762938807/ADAC-eV/KOR/Text/PDF/tesla-model-y-maximum-range_n13ymi.pdf) measures 420 L in the normal covered rear boot, 540 L to the roof, **plus** 105 L below the floor and approximately 80 L in front. Folded rear measurements are 850 L to window height and 1,380 L to the roof. This is a European related configuration, not an Australian homologated measurement, and must remain separately labelled.

[ADAC's measurement explanation](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/autokatalog/autotest/kofferraumvolumen-vergleich-2026/) says it uses defined foam blocks based on ISO 3832, standardises adjustable parts, and measures the normal boot to the cover or lower window edge if there is no cover. This is not evidence that Tesla's 854 L / 822 L are VDA, nor a basis for a universal conversion factor. The ADAC overview and detailed test also differ on folded roof-height totals; do not merge those values.

Evidence gap: a directly applicable Australian VDA rear-boot measurement for either generation was not verified. The practical evidence clearly supports a useful, large family boot, but does not resolve a numeric VDA ranking.

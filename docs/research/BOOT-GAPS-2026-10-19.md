# Boot evidence audit — 19 October 2026

All 35 existing configurations already had a numerical five-seat rear-boot capacity. Missing boot ratings mainly came from excluding every fact whose basis did not contain “VDA”. That hid useful evidence, but simply treating every manufacturer's litre claim as identical would also mislead.

The new `boot-evidence.json` separates the displayed Australian specification from the input used for a space score. Evidence factors are editorial confidence weights, not statistical probabilities. Unknown boundaries, roof-height figures, related markets and unresolved year differences receive less influence and an estimate label. They do not become verified VDA measurements.

## Material findings

- Original Model Y: the [March 2023 ADAC first-party test](https://assets.adac.de/image/upload/Autodatenbank/Autotest/at6259-tesla-model-y-maximum-range-awd/tesla-model-y-maximum-range-awd.pdf), p4, measured 420 L normal rear storage plus 105 L under the load floor. Its Shanghai-built German-market Long Range is related to the Australian configurations. Use the arithmetic 525 L rear-storage proxy at 70% evidence, retaining the 854 L Australian manual headline and separate 117 L frunk. This is not a VDA conversion. The [November 2025 Juniper ADAC test](https://assets.adac.de/image/upload/v1762938807/ADAC-eV/KOR/Text/PDF/tesla-model-y-maximum-range_n13ymi.pdf) supports a separate updated-body record with the same measured rear compartments; retain the Australian 822 L / 116 L published figures.
- The [ADAC method explanation](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/autokatalog/autotest/kofferraumvolumen-vergleich-2026/) standardises seating and loads its normal boot measurement to the luggage-cover lower edge. A different loading height can explain much of the apparent gap between brands. “VDA” on its own does not prove identical loading boundaries.
- [BabyDrive's 2023 original Model Y test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/electric-cars/2023-tesla-model-y/) fitted a twin side-by-side pram plus five bags, or a single pram plus seven. The [related 2021 RAV4 Cruiser test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2021-toyota-rav4-hybrid-cruiser/) also fitted a twin stroller plus five bags, with the load blind covering it. These are specific pram tests, not a conversion between bag counts and litres.
- [WhichCar's Australian T33 guide](https://www.whichcar.com.au/reviews/first-drive/nissan-x-trail-discover-the-full-range-with-the-whichcar-guide) reports **177 L with all seven seats upright**. This fills the sole relevant third-row capacity gap, but the measurement standard and bench position are unspecified. The period Australian manufacturer sheet does not supply this number, so its basis explicitly calls it indicative.
- Australian Nissan e-Power **575 L** and seven-seat petrol **465 L with row three folded** are stated **to roof**. They remain visible but receive only 40% boot-score evidence. The [BabyDrive e-Power test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2023-nissan-x-trail-e-power-hybrid-launch-review/) fitted a twin pram plus three bags; it cannot establish seven-seat petrol boot fit.
- Kluger **552 L** with third row folded and **241 L** with all seven seats up are corroborated as **VDA to beltline** by the [contemporary Australian launch test](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-reviews/toyota-kluger-83805). A related Grande Hybrid [BabyDrive test](https://babydrive.com.au/reviews/suvs/2021-toyota-kluger-hybrid-grande/) also shows a twin pram with all seven seats up. The Australian Toyota numbers remain unchanged.
- Forester S5 **498 L** is explicitly **VDA to cargo cover** in the [Australian MY22 first drive](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-reviews/subaru-forester-85315). This clarifies the method without replacing the manufacturer number with a conflicting mixed-year catalogue entry.
- Current Australian Forester S6 base petrol remains **496 L**; the [Australian MY26 brochure](https://cdn.oem-production.subaru.com.au/documents/Subaru-Forester-brochure.pdf), p53, separates the petrol and hybrid columns. Its method is not labelled VDA, so the score remains an estimate.
- Australian Sealion 6 **425 L** is supported by the [related Netherlands Seal U DM-i manufacturer table](https://www.byd.com/material/byd-site/nl/pdfs/new-pricelist/Prijslijst-BYD-SEAL-U-DM-i-per-24-11-2025_compressed.pdf), p10, which explicitly labels 425–1,440 L VDA for 18.3 kWh models. The Australian extended battery remains a less certain extrapolation; no foreign range, warranty or charging figures were copied.
- [Zeekr Europe's current table](https://www.zeekr.eu/nl-nl/models/7x) identifies **539 L VDA**. Its current updated European powertrains differ from the original Australian cars, so this only corroborates the measurement plausibility; it does not certify the exact Australian loading boundary.
- Five-seat `bootAllRows` entries previously left null as “no third row” now repeat their rear-seats-up capacity, because the UI labels the field “all rows occupied”. No extra storage is invented.

## Files and integration

`boot-gap-updates.json` has 12 unique vehicle patches. Apply them after research-pack vehicle updates. `boot-gap-sources.json` adds eight cited source records. `cargo.ts` exports validated `bootEvidenceRecords` and `bootEvidenceFor(vehicleId)` for the scoring UI. Existing Tesla cargo records and the headline source capacities remain intact.

A score is a compact space indicator. Keep the headline litres, load condition, estimate label and caveat visible. Actual child-seat position and pram dimensions remain the decisive test, especially with a third row occupied.

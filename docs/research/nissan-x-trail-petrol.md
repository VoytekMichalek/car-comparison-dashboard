## Family-fit basis and limits

BabyDrive's seven-seat test used two forward-facing harnessed restraints and a rear-facing capsule. It reports a 186-cm driver fit and wide rear doors. These are positive arrangement, space and loading observations; grade match is related, not exact. A large rear-facing convertible can need different clearance.

The ANCAP **colour matrix was visually inspected**, not inferred from colour-blind PDF extraction. It shows:

- Rear-row centre: sampled capsule, one rear-facing convertible and both sampled boosters cannot fit safely; both sampled forward-facing convertibles fit individually.
- Outboard ISOFIX: rear-facing convertible Model A fails, Model B and capsule pass. Belted installation of the sampled convertibles is a separate result.
- Every tested third-row restraint position is marked installation not allowed. No ISOFIX or top tethers; ANCAP does not recommend restraint installation there.
- The tests are separate individual-seat checks; they do not establish simultaneous three-across fit.

Daily three-seat booster-buckle access stays **unknown**, with no score influence. A related test of harnessed seats does not prove accessible buckles. Centre limitations are visible in the details and comparison evidence.

BabyDrive's later Outlander-manual clarification reports Nissan confirmed that the X-Trail has no analogous left/centre occupancy restriction and permits belt-mounted restraints in all three second-row positions, subject to actual compatibility. This is Nissan's response reported by the reviewer, not a freshly verified Australian owner-manual PDF. No Outlander restriction is transferred to the Nissan.

Third-row access with three row-two restraints requires removing one in the reviewer test. References to sliding occupied restraint seats are not treated as manufacturer permission. The public manuals located during search were European/Maltese or other-market documents and were rejected for Australian installation approval and maintenance intervals. Obtain the actual car's Australian owner manual and maintenance booklet.

## Warranty and servicing

Nissan's terms separate five-year/unlimited-kilometre original cover from service-activated years 6–10, up to 300,000 km. Original retail date and service history matter. The manufacturer explicitly described the end-of-2025 re-entry deadline in September 2025. General terms permit discretionary written Nissan acceptance, so the dashboard does not assert that approval is impossible after the deadline; it requires written VIN-specific status.

Service-activated cover is transferable, with exclusions for normal wear, including suspension, mounts, hoses and exhaust. The current new-vehicle terms state they apply to 2025-on retail, so a 2023–24 car's original warranty booklet remains authoritative for its original-period detail.

A$399 flat-price services are limited to new retail from January 2025 and the first five scheduled visits. They do not automatically price a used 2023–24 purchase. MY26's longer 15,000-km interval is expressly excluded. No original Australian detailed fluid-work PDF was verified; generic forum change intervals and a seller's preventative CVT service are not promoted to Nissan requirements.

## Repair evidence

Official REC-006653 affects **e-POWER variants only**, with a free battery-controller software remedy. It is not charged to this petrol car. Nissan's official VIN recall/service-campaign checker remains an inspection step; failure to find a model-wide petrol notice is not a guarantee of no campaign for a particular VIN.

The generic Australian front damper-pair guide (A$650–1,300 parts/labour) remains clearly labelled a generic scenario, with no T33 frequency claim.

# Hyundai Ioniq 5 — individual evidence audit

Checked 18 October 2026. Covers all four dashboard configurations. The JSON pack contains the mergeable changes and the ten-item audit checklist.

## Material changes

- Corrected the original 72.6-kWh AWD’s sole current A$44,990 asking observation from **drive-away to excluding government charges**. The Carsales detail body explicitly gives EGC. This affects landed acquisition costs, although the advertised amount is unchanged.
- Added a separate facelift servicing allowance of **A$403/year**, rounded from the reported first ten-year schedule: 667 + 689 + 667 + 689 + 1,313 = **A$4,025**. Eight-year averaging misses the expensive tenth-year visit. This remains a low-confidence planning estimate: the published table accompanies an AWD review, and a current RWD VIN quote is still required.
- Added Carsales’ exact MY23 Techniq professional review (**85/100**, explicitly converted to **8.5/10**) and ReDriven’s August 2026 used-car verdict as qualitative family context. ReDriven’s header does not identify a drivetrain, so it is not assigned as an exact AWD review.
- Rechecked the MY23 Australian owner manual directly. General servicing every 24 months / 30,000 km does **not** mean every maintenance item is due only then: low-conductivity coolant is every 36 months / 60,000 km, 12V inspection every 12 months / 15,000 km, brake fluid every 24 months, and standard coolant initially at ten years / 200,000 km. Severe-use provisions can differ.

## Practical family evidence and score distinctions

BabyDrive’s original AWD test is positive evidence for named three-across installation, adult clearance and loading access. It uses two Attain More restraints with an Achieve More centrally. Forward/rear-facing use is described; the article does not establish every three-forward-facing orientation or a three-booster trial. Its 180–186 cm front-occupant observations support the space assessment, rather than vehicle body width.

The Australian MY23 release says the larger battery preserves cabin practicality. The MY23 brochure independently preserves the anchors and rear hip-room dimensions; Dynamiq sliding is manual, Techniq powered. Their three-across evidence is therefore **related**, with reduced influence. The exact Dynamiq family review reports good rear room, but a child struggled to latch the door; this makes access workable rather than an unqualified positive for the MY23 entries.

The facelift’s base 84-kWh Australian family review verifies rear space and the flat footwell. It does not verify a three-restraint arrangement or child-loading access. These remain unknown. Published dimensions do not justify upgrading them.

ANCAP’s PDF was downloaded and its coloured installation table rendered: the selected Type E booster failed the centre position, the selected Type F passed; selected ISOFIX rear-facing convertible Model A also failed. Each restraint is assessed individually, not three together. This adds a specific inspection question; it does not establish whether children can reach three booster buckles. Buckle scores remain unknown for all four configurations.

## Ownership, repairs and reviews

Youi’s own Australian model mean remains A$1,851, rounded to A$1,850. Its November 2025–May 2026 sold-policy cohort and A$1,142–2,234 indicative band do not provide a Queensland household or exact-variant quote. Wider dashboard sensitivity bounds remain estimates.

The original Australian Service Passport verifies the early 12-month / 15,000-km service regime. Prepaid service limits and transfer were checked independently of a lifetime-tariff claim. Old five-year private-use warranty is not retrospectively seven; qualifying post-June-2025 cars require the specified dealer service history for the additional two years. Current battery terms specify 8 years / 160,000 km and SOH below 70% under conditions; used cars require their original contract and service/usage history. Current cover for the auxiliary battery is only two years / 40,000 km.

Official ICCU, park-pawl and driveshaft recall records were read. Applicable recall remedies are free. The Drive article underlying the previously reported Australian 15-year / 300,000-km ICCU extension could not be re-fetched (403), and no fresh public Hyundai Australia VIN contract was located. The pre-existing attributed report remains conditional; overseas extensions and owner posts are not substituted for it.

No defensible Australian model-specific annual fault probability or paid-repair distribution was found. Repair savings remain A$1,200 original AWD; A$1,000 MY23 RWD; A$1,100 MY23 AWD; A$700 facelift. These are low-confidence contingency budgets, not evidence that those amounts will be spent. There is no research basis for artificially increasing or reducing them to force a ranking. ReDriven’s unscoped pooled 2% ICCU assertion is excluded from calculation. Inspection prompts now cover 12V condition, AC/DC charging, underbody/battery enclosure, diagnostic history and tailgate rattles. Neither a battery stress-case cost nor a covered ICCU remedy is treated as routine owner expenditure.

ProductReview remains 3.8/5 from eight contributions, including two identified expert borrowed-car reviews. It is neither a verified-owner-only sample nor a facelift-specific one. Exact Dynamiq and 84-kWh expert reviews remain scoped separately; dynamic article comparison widgets with mismatched variants and charging claims are rejected in favour of manufacturer specifications.

QLD October 2026 private electric-car registration, traffic fee and available Class 1 CTP total A$782.15–798.15; that common electric-car fee group remains correct. Current asking-price depreciation does not establish future resale.

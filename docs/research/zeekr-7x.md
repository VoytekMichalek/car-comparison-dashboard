# Zeekr 7X deep audit — 18 October 2026

Covers both existing Australian configurations: `zeekr7x-rwd-2025` and `zeekr7x-long-range-2025` (2025–26). The household target is **one rear-facing restraint plus two forward-facing seats or boosters**; exact restraint models and adult heights remain unspecified.

## Material findings

- Mixed three-across fit remains **unknown**, rather than being inferred from exterior width or adult seating space. There is good independent evidence of useful 90-degree rear-door opening on the actual standard RWD.
- Found a meaningful provenance problem: the community-hosted manual describes front-passenger ISOFIX/top-tether/airbag-switch equipment that the Australian ANCAP report says is absent. It must not be treated as an exact Australian anchor map.
- Found first-person mixed-seat accounts, but they do not establish the target arrangement safely. A powered-door car appears to be Performance; one contributor names Britax b-grow ClickTight seats while saying one was flipped rear-facing. Britax's own manual identifies that restraint as forward-facing-only. The account may have omitted or misidentified a restraint. It is excluded from positive fit scoring.
- Added a scoped standard-RWD observed consumption fact: CarsGuide reported **15.6 kWh/100 km over 2,383 km/three months**. Meter/charging boundary is unspecified. Its calculated 455 km range is **not** a measured full-battery range and is explicitly not populated as an observed range.
- Added a **standard-RWD planning consumption override of 18.2 kWh/100 km**, anchored to RACV's 14 October 2026 two-week city/country test. Battery-side treatment is an explicit budgeting assumption, not a verified meter result; broad **15.6–23** bounds and low confidence remain. Household charging losses are applied once. Long Range retains the existing 20 proxy because a matched, documented Australian consumption boundary was not verified.
- Retained the insurance, tyres, service and repair savings estimates after checking their anchors. No defensible annual repair-cost dataset justifies increasing the RWD fund because Performance has air suspension or automatic doors.

## Item-by-item audit

### Pricing

Re-opened all three included current stock advertisements. Standard RWD: **A$64,109 drive-away**, new 2026 Queensland car, 30 km, Forest Green/black PU, Carsales `OAG-AD-26350362`. Long Range: **A$71,805 drive-away each**, new 2026 stock **HZ2033** (0 km, VIN ending 038633, grey) and **HZ2137** (30 km, VIN ending 043589, Brook Blue/silver roof, black/grey interior). Distinct physical VINs; no sold/unavailable label observed. The LR advertisements are closely related regional dealer stock, not independent geographical market coverage.

These are optioned stock asks, **not** transaction values, used-car medians or proof the base RRPs changed. The July CarExpert list-price table remains A$57,900 RWD / A$63,900 LR before on-roads. RWD n=1 is low confidence; LR n=2 remains a narrow medium-confidence observation. Broader used/demonstrator market coverage remains a gap; new and demo stock should not be mixed in a single median.

### Powertrain and consumption

Re-read Australian maker page and brochure. Standard RWD is **75 kWh LFP / 480 km WLTP target / 450 kW conditional DC**; LR is **100 kWh NCM / 615 km target / 420 kW conditional DC**. Both list 22 kW AC, requiring appropriate three-phase infrastructure to reach the maximum. Best-case 10–80% claims remain 13/16 minutes; these are conditional engineering claims, not everyday Australian charger guarantees. Do not project China-market 900 V/facelift specifications onto these cars.

Manufacturer Australian consumption with a verified energy boundary remains unverified. The CarsGuide observation is standard RWD only; the newer RACV report returned 18.2 kWh/100 km, a realistic 420 km in its use and a 204 kW observed charging peak during one 30–100% session. Those charging/range observations do not establish the car's maximum or a controlled depletion result. No matched AAA Real-World Testing result was located. LR CarExpert's 17.7 claimed-consumption table is secondary reporting without a documented energy boundary and was not imported as an authoritative specification.

### Family fit, ventilation and grade scope

Australian ANCAP confirms **two outer rear ISOFIX positions and three rear top tethers**. Its installation procedure assesses each restraint separately; full marks do not establish a simultaneous three-across combination. The report also says no qualified child-presence detection system; the cabin camera must not be described as equivalent certification.

Standard-RWD CarsGuide long-term testing confirms rear doors opening 90 degrees and useful B-pillar ventilation. Reviewers find generous adult leg/headroom and a mostly flat rear floor. These observations help loading and adult comfort but do **not** establish front-seat clearance behind the household's actual rear-facing restraint. Daily booster-buckle access also remains untested.

Manufacturer luggage claim remains 539 L with the rear row available; measurement/loading standard is not specified. The 62 L front compartment is a separate water-capacity claim for RWD, not extra interchangeable rear boot volume. Boot/pram trial remains necessary.

Grade distinctions: both RWD grades use conventional suspension, PU upholstery and a powered roof shade. **AWD automatic doors, air suspension and powered rear-window sunshades are not standard on the listed RWD/LR cars.** LR adds front-seat cooling/massage and other equipment; base RWD lacks cooled seats, relevant in Queensland. Do not generalise a flagship China-market family demonstration.

### Warranty

Read the policy linked directly by Zeekr Australia's current service page. Private-use basic cover is **5 years/unlimited km**; traction battery **8 years/160,000 km**, including a 70% original-capacity threshold, with qualifying repair/replacement/reconditioning remedies. Original auxiliary/key-fob batteries, lamps and alignment have shorter limits; service records and exclusions matter. Australian Consumer Law rights are additional. Original 2025 delivery/promotional contract and used-buyer balance/eligibility still require written confirmation; the current policy is not proof every original contract was identical.

### Servicing

The reproduced September 2025 Zeekr statement recommends 24 months/40,000 km, with optional interim health checks. Its table shows **A$605 at year 2 and A$1,150 at year 4**, plus optional year 1 free / year 3 A$330 / year 5 A$300. Basic scheduled total **A$1,755**, all checks **A$2,385** over five years. Coolant/reducer/brake-fluid work explains why the fourth-year visit is much dearer than an interim check.

The current maker service landing page has no public retail tariff. July CarExpert and October RACV both could not confirm updated pricing. Retained **A$500/year, A$350–650**, low confidence; do not promise launch free-check eligibility. The community-hosted manual p375 has normal-use 24m/40,000km and reduced brake-fluid/filter intervals in harsh conditions, but its market mismatch means the actual VIN-specific Australian instructions must be obtained. Do not use an overseas Malaysian service PDF as an Australian schedule.

### Insurance

Read Youi's actual 7X-specific policy data: **A$1,520–3,026**, policies sold 22 November 2025–22 May 2026. Youi states 80% fell **within or below** this range; it does not label this as a mean/median or middle-80% interval. Retained **A$2,270** as rounded midpoint planning assumption and A$1,520–4,000 sensitivity. This mixes national customers/trims and is not this household's postcode/driver/excess quote. Forum claims of an insurer refusing the whole brand are not substituted for this actual insurer evidence.

### Tyres

JAX currently lists Zeekr-marked **HL255/50R19 110V XL ZK ELT Pirelli P Zero PZ4 SUV A$379 each fitted**, including balancing, valve, disposal and GST: **A$1,516/set**. A current four-buy promotion is A$303.20 each (A$1,212.80/set); retain the ordinary reference rather than assume the promotion exists when replacement is due. Life **35,000 km** remains assumed, not a model-specific measured average; at 15,000 km/year the default works out about **A$650/year**. Placard minimum load/speed, actual wheels and local stock/quote remain necessary; an HL retail product is not proof of a minimum legal placard rating.

### Repairs and recalls

Retained **A$900/year savings reserve, A$450–2,000 sensitivity, low confidence**, for both RWD grades. It is a planning buffer, not an observed average failure bill. Basic/battery warranty coverage, vehicle age and developing local support are relevant, but lack of history is not proof of poor reliability. No matched Australian installed traction-pack/electronic/strut quotation or credible annual failure-cost distribution was verified. Existing battery/suspension stress-case numbers remain generic estimates, not new quoted 7X costs.

No indexed Australian 7X recall notice was found by exact-brand/model searches. The official register opened, but its dynamic results could not be independently verified through this fetch; this is **not a guarantee of no recalls**. Get VIN-specific open recalls/service campaigns from Zeekr. Online software-delivery-delay stories are not labelled an official recall. Insured crash/glass costs and AWD air-suspension/automatic-door stories are not folded into the RWD defect reserve.

### Reviews and support

Existing CarsGuide **8.3/10** is scoped to standard RWD, three months and 2,383 km; it is a professional opinion, not a long-term reliability survey. Added fresh, unrated RACV RWD commentary: packaging/comfort/refinement/charging positives, screen dependence and voice limitations negatives. The CarExpert LR introduction explicitly says the extended evaluation and updated service pricing are still pending; no completed reliability verdict is invented.

ProductReview currently shows **2.0/5 from 17** on a mixed Zeekr **dealership/brand** page with X/7X and purchase/delivery accounts. It is deliberately **excluded** as a 7X owner score. Selection bias and developing Australian fleet age prevent meaningful cross-brand failure-rate inference.

### Ownership, safety and fees

ANCAP 2025 five-star applicability is all Australian variants from August-2025 builds/September-2025 sales: 91% adult and 87% child. ROVER records 2,000 kg braked / 750 kg unbraked for both rear-drive variants, but explicitly labels the data **applicant supplied and not independently verified/approved by the department**. Revised wording removes the impression the page itself certifies a loaded tow combination.

Queensland's 1 October 2026 private-use EV fee group is **A$782.15–798.15 including Class 1 CTP** (A$303.10 registration + A$67.25 traffic fee + A$411.80–427.80 CTP). Existing midpoint A$790.15 is consistent. Depreciation/resale remains scenario modelling, not a verified 10-year forecast. Inspect tyres, underbody/pack impact damage, charging, all door/emergency releases, actual seat/anchor layout, rear HVAC, software version and written service/warranty balance.

## Score implications

A three-seat-fit score should not be inflated merely because two ISOFIX sets and three tethers exist. The arrangement, buckles and rear-facing clearance criteria remain unknown with no positive contribution; independently evidenced loading access may contribute. RWD is exact for the practical access review; LR uses related standard-RWD practical evidence. No artificial Tesla/RAV4/Zeekr ranking is imposed. Insurance/repair/tyre assumptions retain weak influence; a future documented household installation or quote can replace them.

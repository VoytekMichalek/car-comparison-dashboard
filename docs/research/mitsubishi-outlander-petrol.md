# Mitsubishi Outlander petrol — deep audit

Checked 18 October 2026. Configuration: `outlander-ls-petrol-fwd-2022`, Australian original ZM MY22–MY24 LS, 2.5 petrol CVT, FWD, seven seats. This is not an AWD, PHEV, previous ZL or MY25 facelift assessment.

## Material findings

- The requested **one rear-facing plus two forward-facing seats or boosters remains unconfirmed**. BabyDrive’s related 2022 Exceed shows three rear-facing seats, and separately three forward-facing seats. The rear-facing photograph was inspected; its caption is consistent with three rear-facing restraints. This is not an explicit mixed LS test.
- A newer BabyDrive investigation reports an Outlander manual caution about the second-row left seating position beside a centre restraint. It records conflicting Mitsubishi media/customer-relations interpretations and no final clarification. The published caution screenshot was read, but its exact model-year/powertrain scope is not established. This audit does **not** declare every Outlander prohibited. Exact Australian petrol manual and written Mitsubishi clarification are still needed. Family arrangement and booster-buckle grades remain unknown, so no family-fit score is assigned.
- Related Exceed rear-facing clearance is asymmetric: a 186 cm passenger fitted, but the 162 cm reviewer only just fitted in the driver’s seat with row two fully back. The dashboard now makes that distinction. Front clearance must be checked with the actual restraints.
- ANCAP’s colour-coded matrix was visually inspected: belted convertible Model A is red in the centre in both orientations; Type E 4–8 booster is also red there. Model B and Type F are green. Outboard positions are green for the selected restraints. These are separate installation checks, not a simultaneous three-across approval.
- Row three has neither ISOFIX nor top tethers. ANCAP does not recommend child restraints there. BabyDrive’s untethered folding-booster suggestion is retained only as a source limitation, not promoted as manufacturer approval. With three row-two restraints installed, the related test needed to uninstall one before folding for third-row access.

## Warranty and servicing

Primary manufacturer warranty page and October 2023 full terms were downloaded and read. Base cover 5 years/100,000 km; eligible extension to 10 years/200,000 km requires all scheduled services on time in the authorised dealer network. Cover transfers with vehicle ownership, not with a restarted clock. Some government, rental and national business customers are excluded. Original VIN booklet/retail date matters.

Limited-life items including injectors, shocks/struts, hoses, rubber/plastic components and lamps have 12-month/20,000-km terms in the October 2023 document; 12 V battery is 12 months. Australian Consumer Law is separate. Do not equate the ten-year headline with blanket component cover.

MY24 primary schedule closes the previous explicit gap. A$450/year remains a low-confidence long-ownership estimate, not a fixed cap or guaranteed remaining-service cost. A used purchase starts partway through the schedule; eligible unexpired caps transfer. Severe-use extras, wear, catch-up and repairs cost extra. Current new-purchase promotions are not applied retroactively.

MY23 manufacturer maintenance PDF was read, including visual inspection of the fluid table: CVT fluid 90,000 km / 72 months normal; 45,000 km / 36 months severe. Spark plugs 90,000 km / 72 months. Coolant first 150,000 km / eight years, then 75,000 km / four years. Brake fluid 30,000 km / 24 months. Fuel filter 180,000 km / 144 months. Severe-use engine oil/filter every 7,500 km; heavy city traffic in heat, towing and rental use are among listed conditions. Confirm the exact car’s schedule and operating classification; no assumption of AWD transfer/differential servicing for FWD.

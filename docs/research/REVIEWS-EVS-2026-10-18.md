# EV review expansion — 18 October 2026

Six model families, all 16 existing EV configurations checked. The companion dataset is `src/data/review-expansion/evs.json`. It adds 30 owner/professional cohorts from 39 source records. Figures are a dated snapshot of the readable primary pages, not a claim to have manually read every submission.

The strongest new owner pools are large: CARFAX shows 611 reviews for the 2023 Model Y, 414 for the 2022 EV6 and 302 for the 2022 Ioniq 5. Those are US model-wide cohorts, with reduced relevance to Australian configurations. They are not Australian defect statistics. Publisher contributions must never be described as a count of distinct people across websites.

## Counting and scoring

- Record a publisher's exact denominator: reviews, ratings or survey responses. A comment count or a reader's vote on the usefulness of an article is not a vehicle review or vehicle rating.
- Reduce international matches for hardware, equipment, country and support differences. New generations, unresolved drivetrains and incompatible cohorts receive factor zero.
- `overlapGroup` connects reused year pages and related family pools. In addition, the app should count at most one owner contribution per publisher for a configuration. It must not pretend to remove people who post on several websites.
- Keep professional review indexes separate from owner feedback. Use the media subscore from The Car Expert, not its all-in score, which already contains safety, running costs and warranty.
- Reference-only cohorts can display a published figure and scope without changing the configuration score. A large incompatible count is not more relevant than a small matching one.
- Self-selected owner ratings can identify inspection questions and sentiment. They cannot provide an annual failure probability, a repair bill forecast or proof that every reported incident occurred as described.

## Tesla Model Y

Opened [CARFAX 2023](https://www.carfax.com/Research/Tesla/Model-Y/2023), [Edmunds 2023](https://www.edmunds.com/tesla/model-y/2023/consumer-reviews/), [KBB 2023](https://www.kbb.com/tesla/model-y/2023/consumer-reviews/) and the corresponding [KBB 2026](https://www.kbb.com/tesla/model-y/2026/consumer-reviews/) page. CARFAX rates 4.8/5, Edmunds 3.9/5 and KBB 4.2/5. Retain this disagreement; it is evidence of selection and publisher differences, not a reason to choose the most flattering site.

KBB's 2023 and 2026 pages expose the same 89-review distribution and even a January 2021 purchase. Record it once and do not call it a Juniper cohort. Edmunds' original page also contains an account of a 2021 purchase, despite the 2023 title.

The [Edmunds 2026 page](https://www.edmunds.com/tesla/model-y/2026/consumer-reviews/) shows 4.3/5 from 33 reviews. Several explicitly discuss Juniper/Launch AWD, but one ownership chronology is inconsistent with an exclusively refreshed sample. Its relevance to the Australian refreshed RWD is limited. Comments about US FSD subscriptions, charging connectors or tax credits do not establish Australian equipment or costs.

[The Car Expert](https://www.thecarexpert.co.uk/tesla-model-y-2022/) gives a 77/100 media subscore and explicitly mixes original/facelift versions. No verified denominator is imported.

## Explicit remaining gaps

No claimed cross-platform total is a unique-owner count. No sample is treated as a controlled statistical reliability survey. A suitable 50+ sample was not found for every battery, drivetrain and model update; where broad cohorts exist but cannot match, the limitation is carried into both score relevance and display copy. Recheck publisher counts and scope before future scoring changes.

Audited all three existing configurations: original RWD 2022–24, original Long Range AWD 2023–24, and refreshed RWD/Premium RWD 2025–26. The companion JSON contains ten item-level findings and an evidence assessment for each configuration.

- Added AAA original Long Range consumption **16.7 kWh/100 km** and Juniper RWD **14.8 kWh/100 km**, measured from mains recharge energy. The calculator must not add another charging-loss allowance to either number. Observed ranges **490 km** and **450 km** remain distinct from the **533 km** and **466 km** lab claims. - Added an original-AWD professional review. Its current categorical verdict is preserved; no numeric rating is invented.
- Corrected the original RWD price snapshot geography to the two included dealers. All seven individual asks were reopened; the price points and physical-car counts stay unchanged. The private AWD ad currently lists no roadworthy certificate and June 2023 compliance.

## What the family evidence establishes

The original RWD has specific positive practical evidence: a named InfaSecure three-restraint combination, an alternative combination, a flat floor and good front-seat clearance. The refreshed RWD has its own separate successful mixed-facing test. These support a cabin-room strength, while **three daily booster buckles remain unverified**. Both generations' ANCAP technical reports document a centre-position incompatibility with one selected booster and a capsule.

The RAV4 comparison is nuanced. BabyDrive's 2021 RAV4 test also successfully installed three forward-facing seats. Its 180 cm driver/capsule observation and Model Y's 186 cm driver/Attain More observation use different restraints and are not a controlled width measurement. A 2024 Australian original-AWD review explicitly finds Model Y's rear door aperture narrower than RAV4's. Original Model Y access therefore receives “workable”, despite good occupied cabin room. Juniper access uses its own positive flat-floor/access observation; the old body's door limitation is not silently assigned to the refresh.

There is no evidence here that every three-seat combination fits, or that body width determines restraint compatibility. The user's exact forward-facing/booster arrangement still needs a fitting trial.

## Warranty, repairs and inspection scope

Original 2022–2025 basic cover was four years/80,000 km. New 2026 deliveries get five years/unlimited kilometres under normal-use terms; this does not restart an older vehicle's cover. RWD battery/drive-unit cover is eight years/160,000 km, AWD eight years/192,000 km, with 70% retention. Original contract, delivery date and exclusions govern. The extra Tesla used-car warranty is for Tesla Certified Pre-Owned purchases. Repair-parts warranty is separate.

A current Australian fitted upper-arm-pair offer is A$1,823; a commercial inspection guide gives a rough compressor exposure of A$2,000–4,000+. Neither supplies a representative failure probability. German TÜV inspection defects and ADAC roadside incidents measure different events, and neither can honestly be converted into Australian paid-repair dollars. **No better empirical annual repair average was found.** The existing low-confidence repair funds should therefore remain estimates. Insured collision claims and free recall/warranty remedies must not be counted again as routine owner-paid repairs.

Official Australian recall records were checked, including original-body steering, bonnet warning, tail lamps and cold-weather steering, and the refreshed driver-window campaign. These are VIN-specific checks. The official Tesla recall browse list showed no newer Australian Model Y campaign; this does not prove a particular VIN is clear. Inspect underside damage, suspension noise, inner-edge tyre wear, HVAC operation, AC/DC charging and software/recall completion.

## Remaining evidence gaps

Tesla does not consistently publish pack gross/usable capacity; independent reports mix measures. The current en_au towing documentation contains conflicting North American tables, so a local plated towing number is still withheld. Exact Australian late-2023 suspension/hardware cutover remains unresolved: overseas announcements and first-hand Performance tests do not prove a RWD VIN change. Existing dimensions/boot measures were checked against Tesla manuals, with Tesla cargo litres kept separate from VDA litres. Late stock listing claims do not establish unchanged 2025–26 equipment.

# Evidence gaps, PHEVs and repair accounting — 19 October 2026

## Existing catalogue

All existing configurations had a sourced rear-boot capacity. A strict VDA-only scoring rule, rather than missing volumes, caused many empty boot scores. The score now separates published litres from a comparable or clearly estimated scoring input. Tesla keeps its Australian 854/822 L headline and front storage, while a related ADAC test provides a labelled 525 L rear-storage proxy for scoring. Other measurement boundaries, row conditions and related-market figures have explicit evidence factors. No VDA conversions are fabricated. See [boot/specification audit](BOOT-GAPS-2026-10-19.md).

Five-seat “all rows occupied” now means the same seats-up boot, instead of an irrelevant missing third-row value. The seven-seat X-Trail adds the locally reported indicative 177 L all-seats-up figure. Every car retains child-seat anchors, installation observations and an explicit personal fit-check status. New Sealion 6 related-test photography establishes a rear-facing capsule plus two forward-facing seats; it does not establish every Maxi-Cosi/booster combination. See [family-fit audit](FAMILY-FIT-GAPS-2026-10-19.md).

The app no longer uses a generic “No data” category label. It says what is actually unresolved: “Fit check required”, “Unrated” or “Not yet rated”, with the relevant facts and sources alongside it. An official Unrated vehicle is not assigned invented ANCAP stars. Unknown daily booster-buckle access is not assumed good, and individual ANCAP restraint installations are not treated as simultaneous three-across tests. Missing observations contribute no fabricated score.

## Repair savings and spending

Repair savings are off by default, including older saved notebooks that lack the new setting. A clearly labelled checkbox on Ownership costs and individual cost panels can add optional savings to the annual/period **budget**. The setting persists locally and exports/imports with the notebook. It is not added to ownership **spending**, even when that budget view is enabled.

- Everyday spending = energy + comprehensive insurance + registration/CTP + scheduled servicing + tyres + finance interest/fees.
- Optional budget = everyday spending + chosen repair savings.
- Ownership spending = everyday spending × years + purchase + setup − resale + one entered uncovered repair event.
- The repair event is counted once. The optional fund is cash retained until spent, so it is not deducted from a repair bill and also charged as expenditure.
- Scenario shortfall uses only enabled savings, explicitly assuming contributions build from zero over the full period. An earlier fault needs more cash.

Running-budget scores always use everyday spending and its evidence. Moving the savings switch or entering a different fund contribution does not change a score or its evidence coverage.

## Future repair exposure rating

Added a ninth importance category, default importance **5/10**, adjustable from 1–10. Old eight-value shared links and backups retain their original weights and receive the new default.

This is a **provisional editorial exposure estimate**, not actuarial expected cost or reliability. It uses the existing researched configuration allowance as a rough ordinal reference for age, cover and uncovered component prices: A$500 reference → 10; A$1,500 → 1, linear and capped. Its evidence factor is only **25%** because comparable Australian model-specific paid-failure frequencies are unavailable. Your selected savings amount cannot improve the score. Uncertainty is a limit on influence, not evidence of failures. The formula and source limitations remain visible.

Excluded: insured crash/fire/flood/theft/glass damage; covered warranty defects; eligible free recalls; applicable consumer-law remedies; scheduled servicing and ordinary tyre replacement. Insured windscreen and routine brake examples cannot automatically populate the mechanical repair scenario. Before entering a repair event, use the amount actually payable after applicable remedies. An insurance excess can be planned separately; a complete covered claim cost is not a mechanical bill.

Primary scope references: [NRMA insurance explanation](https://www.nrma.com.au/car-insurance/car-insurance-faqs/what-is-car-insurance), [ACCC car consumer guarantees](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/new-and-second-hand-cars). These establish coverage distinctions, not model-specific failure rates.

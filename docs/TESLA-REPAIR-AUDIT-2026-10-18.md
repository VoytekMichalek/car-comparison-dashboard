# Tesla repair-cost cross-check

Checked 18 October 2026. Australian Model Y, especially the original 2022–24 body. This is an independent check of the repair evidence, not an actuarial estimate.

The current A$1,800 annual original-RWD reserve is already the largest default in the catalogue. The evidence supports a meaningful suspension and thermal-system contingency, but does **not** establish that Tesla owners pay the most in annual mechanical repairs. A high individual bill, frequent roadside calls, insurance repair costs and an annual repair average measure different things.

## Sources excluded from numeric estimates

- [Whiteline's cost article](https://whitelineperformance.com/au/tesla-model-y-control-arm-replacement-cost) quotes much lower arm-pair prices than its live WA533 product offer and uses miles. Use the live itemised offer for that product; its narrative is not a measured failure survey.
- [Rodz's A$249 auxiliary-battery starting price](https://rodz.com.au/services/tesla-service) covers a mixed Tesla range and describes all Teslas as using 12-V batteries. This is insufficient for a 2022+ Australian Model Y lithium low-voltage replacement. [Tesla's Australian manual](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/2020_2024_modely/en_au/GUID-3567D5F4-A5F4-4323-8BE0-023D5438FFC6.html) distinguishes the batteries and says Shanghai vehicles built after approximately October 2021 use lithium-ion. Confirm the actual vehicle's information screen.
- [Jaunt's A$5,499 including GST drive-unit coolant-delete service](https://www.jauntmotors.com/post/why-were-fixing-teslas-now) is an actual Australian workshop offer, but explicitly concerns **2012–20 Model S/X large drive units**. It cannot price a Model Y failure.
- Generic vehicle repair templates, foreign currency estimates, unsighted owner invoices and accident-panel-repair bills are excluded from annual mechanical-repair estimates. One generic Model Y brake calculator even suggests some examples have rear drums, demonstrating why a model name in a page title is insufficient verification.

## Warranty and owner-paid exposure

[Tesla's current Australian support page](https://www.tesla.com/en_AU/support/Vehicle-warranty) explicitly says terms depend on delivery date and the vehicle's own document governs. It permits independent non-warranty repair. It distinguishes original vehicle cover from later paid-repair parts cover: current Tesla-supplied/installed HV battery and drive-unit repairs have four-year/80,000-km terms, while screens/MCUs have two-year/40,000-km terms. These do not apply automatically to salvage parts.

For original cars, the [historical Australian warranty](https://www.tesla.com/sites/default/files/New_Vehicle_Limited_Warranty_Australia_en_AU.pdf) has four-year/80,000-km basic cover. Current five-year/unlimited cover does not retrospectively restart it. Battery/drive-unit cover can remain after basic cover expires. Obtain the actual coverage record before assuming the owner pays for a defect. Free recall rectification and manufacturing-warranty jobs should not be counted again as paid repairs.

## Interpretation and recommended planning defaults

The repair reserve is **money set aside**, not an annual bill forecast. A defensible app should show the evidence and uncertainty rather than imply the ranking is an observed Australian cost league table.

Recommended editorial defaults, if retaining a single flat annual saving amount:

| Configuration | Annual reserve | Low/high saving scenarios | Rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Original Model Y RWD | **Keep A$1,800** | **A$700–3,500** | Preserves the selected public budget; broadens a previously narrow A$2,000 high scenario to acknowledge lumpy multi-thousand-dollar work. |
| Original Model Y Long Range | **A$1,800** | **A$800–3,500** | Same-age chassis/thermal exposure. A lower reserve than RWD lacks a clear evidential basis. A second motor alone does not quantify a higher failure cost, particularly while drive-unit cover remains. |
| Juniper RWD | **A$1,000** | **A$350–2,500** | An explicitly cautious long-ownership budget: more remaining basic cover reduces early cash exposure, but a ten-year holding period extends beyond cover. No long-term Juniper failure-rate evidence supports precision. |

These proposed amounts are **analyst saving assumptions**, not numbers directly calculated from repair incidence. No Australian representative model/year dataset with failure probabilities and owner-paid bills was found. There is no credible basis here to multiply all Tesla costs simply to ensure it looks worst. If the calculator later models years under cover separately, it should apply that same method to every model and preserve personal overrides.

Roadside reliability and inspection evidence are reviewed separately in the release audit: [ADAC 2026](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/unfall-schaden-panne/adac-pannenstatistik-2026/) rates the Model Y favourably for breakdowns, while the [TÜV 2026 report](https://www.tuev-verband.de/pressemitteilungen/tuev-report-2026) flags suspension/brakes/lights at German roadworthiness inspections. Neither supplies Australian repair dollars, and neither is evidence for Juniper's long-term record.
